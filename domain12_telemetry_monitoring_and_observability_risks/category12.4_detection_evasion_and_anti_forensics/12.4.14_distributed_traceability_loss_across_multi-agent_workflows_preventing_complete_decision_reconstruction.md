# 12.4.14 Distributed Traceability Loss Across Multi-Agent Workflows Preventing Complete Decision Reconstruction [NoFW]

Multi-agent workflow decisions are logged in per-agent databases with independent schemas, different retention periods, and without explicit causal linkage between agent handoffs; regulatory compliance investigations that require complete decision reconstruction cannot achieve the audit trail coverage needed to demonstrate that protected characteristics played no role in adverse decisions when 30–50% of the decision chain is unavailable due to retention policy deletions, schema incompatibilities, and missing cross-agent causal links.

For example, a regulatory investigation into hiring bias requires reconstructing decision chains for 10,000 candidate evaluations across 20 resume-screening agents, but 90-day retention policies have deleted 18% of records, three different storage backends (PostgreSQL, MongoDB, Elasticsearch) require separate extraction procedures with incompatible schema mappings, and 35% of records cannot be joined to demographic data due to consent-based segregated storage—leaving investigators with only 48% decision chain coverage, insufficient to conclusively demonstrate non-discrimination under burden-shifting regulatory frameworks where incomplete evidence is presumed adverse.

Standard defenses such as data retention policy extensions, schema standardization requirements, and audit log mandates are insufficient in multi-agent deployments because schema evolution across independently deployed agent versions creates retroactive incompatibilities, distributed storage selection across agent types prevents unified query access, and demographic data segregation requirements conflict with the complete record linkage needed for bias analysis across agent boundaries.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Single-agent decision systems maintain all records in a unified schema with one retention policy, enabling complete audit trail reconstruction from a single data store; multi-agent distributed decision logging fragments across heterogeneous storage backends with independent schemas and retention configurations, creating structural gaps in audit coverage that individually appear as acceptable operational trade-offs but collectively prevent the complete decision reconstruction required for regulatory compliance and fairness investigation.
