# 12.4.8 OpenTelemetry Metrics Aggregation Exposing Multi-Agent Operational Patterns Through Centralized Observability Infrastructure [NoFW]

Centralized OpenTelemetry dashboards aggregating metrics from all agents expose fleet-wide dependency patterns, tool invocation frequencies, cost structures, and performance headroom to any adversary who accesses the observability system; this intelligence enables precise attack calibration (targeting during peak hours, sizing denial-of-wallet attacks to known cost structures) that would be unavailable from monitoring a single agent.

Centralized observability infrastructure exploits the architectural property that consolidated multi-agent dashboards produce comprehensive operational intelligence as a side effect of their legitimate purpose: an adversary accessing OpenTelemetry dashboards learns that the fleet makes 200,000 external API calls daily with 45-call-per-second peaks during business hours, that summarization operations account for 84% of monthly cost at $84,000, and that specific tool invocations average $0.42 per call against a $500 daily budget cap. With this intelligence, the attacker submits 1,200 crafted queries that each trigger the expensive tool, exhausting the daily budget in 14 minutes and rendering the entire agent fleet unavailable for the remaining 23 hours—a denial-of-wallet attack calibrated to the exact cost structure disclosed by the centralized dashboard. Alternatively, knowing from time-series data that ArXiv dependency usage spikes 3x during business hours, the attacker times a targeted dependency degradation attack to coincide with the peak load period when the fleet has the least margin to absorb disruption.

Standard defenses such as role-based dashboard permissions and monitoring system access controls are insufficient because they protect the monitoring interface but do not reduce the intelligence value of the aggregated data once access is obtained; the comprehensive multi-agent view creates attack planning intelligence that is unavailable from compromising any single agent's metrics directly.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Accessing a single agent's telemetry reveals that agent's local operational patterns with bounded intelligence about one deployment's resource consumption and dependency usage; accessing centralized multi-agent observability infrastructure exposes comprehensive fleet-wide attack surfaces—exact dependency traffic volumes enabling infrastructure targeting, per-agent performance gaps enabling targeted amplification of existing degradation, total cost structures enabling denial-of-wallet calibration, and optimization weaknesses revealing performance gaps that planned improvements have not yet addressed.
