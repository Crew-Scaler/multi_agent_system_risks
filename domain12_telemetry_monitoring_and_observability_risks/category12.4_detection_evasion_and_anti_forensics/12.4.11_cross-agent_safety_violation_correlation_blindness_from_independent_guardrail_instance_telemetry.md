# 12.4.11 Cross-Agent Safety Violation Correlation Blindness from Independent Guardrail Instance Telemetry [NoFW]

Per-agent guardrail systems log safety violations to independent telemetry streams; a distributed jailbreak campaign submitting 2–3 attempts per agent across a 45-agent fleet generates per-agent violation rates of approximately 1% (below individual agent anomaly thresholds) while constituting a 100-attempt coordinated campaign that would be detectable only through cross-fleet violation correlation.

For example, an attacker operates 20 compromised user accounts each submitting exactly 5 jailbreak attempts distributed across different agents so that each account appears in at most 2 agents' individual logs; per-agent violation rates stay at approximately 1%, below every individual agent's 3% anomaly threshold, while fleet-wide 100 attempts from a single coordinated campaign go entirely undetected because no system aggregates violation telemetry across agent-scoped boundaries to reveal the temporal clustering and source attribution pattern that would identify the campaign.

Standard defenses such as per-agent anomaly detection, per-user rate limiting, and guardrail model updates are insufficient because they analyze violation streams in isolation; an attacker who distributes attempts below per-agent detection thresholds exploits the structural absence of cross-agent correlation infrastructure, and updating guardrail content classifiers addresses individual attempt content without addressing the coordination-blindness architectural gap that allows distributed campaigns to hide below per-agent detection thresholds indefinitely.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In single-agent deployments all guardrail violations flow through one telemetry context enabling volume, pattern, and source attribution analysis within one data stream; in multi-agent independent guardrail instances each agent's violation stream is a separate observability unit with its own threshold calibration, and coordinated campaigns that distribute below per-agent thresholds exploit the structural inability to assert fleet-wide safety posture from isolated per-agent metrics—making cross-agent attribution of coordinated jailbreak campaigns structurally impossible without centralized violation correlation.
