# 12.2.60 Retry Sequence Telemetry Missing Attack Indicators [NoFW]

Retry telemetry records retry counts and success rates but fails to capture retry sequence characteristics indicating attacks; in multi-agent systems, specific retry patterns such as rapid-succession retries, retries on non-retryable errors, or simultaneous retries from multiple agents may indicate attacks, but standard telemetry recording "operation retried 3 times then succeeded" misses the detailed sequence revealing attack patterns.

For example, an attacker triggers simultaneous coordinated retries across five agents against the same authentication endpoint, producing a distributed credential-stuffing pattern that each individual agent's retry telemetry logs as isolated "authentication retry, eventual success" events without any cross-agent sequence reconstruction.

Standard defenses such as per-agent retry-count limits and exponential backoff enforcement are insufficient because they regulate individual retry behavior without capturing the cross-agent timing and sequencing of retry patterns that distinguish coordinated attack sequences from coincidental simultaneous service degradation.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In a single-agent system, retry sequence characteristics are contained within one agent's event stream and can be analyzed for temporal patterns indicating attack; in multi-agent systems, coordinated retry attacks are deliberately distributed across agents to fall below per-agent detection thresholds, making cross-agent retry sequence correlation the necessary detection unit—which standard telemetry architectures do not implement.
