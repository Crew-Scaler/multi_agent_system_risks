# 12.2.68 Confidence Score Utility Basis Opacity [NoFW]

Confidence scores presented to users or in approval workflows often derive from expected utility calculations but telemetry does not reveal the calculation basis; users and auditors see "87% confidence" without understanding that it represents a misidentified utility value, and in multi-agent systems confidence aggregation obscures which agents' utility calculations contributed, enabling specific high-weight agents' utilities to be poisoned affecting overall confidence without detection.

For example, an attacker poisons the utility weighting of a high-influence research agent, causing it to assign inflated expected utility to a flawed hypothesis; the downstream confidence aggregator synthesizes reports from multiple agents and presents "91% confidence" in the flawed hypothesis, with no telemetry capturing which agent's utility calculation drove the aggregate score upward or why.

Standard defenses such as confidence threshold gates and human approval workflows are insufficient because they act on aggregated confidence values that appear legitimately high, while the underlying utility calculation defect remains opaque behind the scalar confidence output that monitoring systems and human reviewers see.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Confidence aggregation across agents is a structural amplifier of utility-function attacks: a single compromised agent with high contribution weight can shift aggregate confidence without triggering per-agent anomalies, and the absence of per-agent utility contribution logging makes forensic attribution after a decision failure practically impossible.
