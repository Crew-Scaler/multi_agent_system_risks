# 12.2.47 Rollout Policy Drift as Unobserved Behavior Change [NoFW]

Adaptive MCTS rollout policies that learn improved heuristics over time drift gradually without explicit monitoring; adversaries can steer rollout policy evolution toward attacker-preferred behaviors through selective input crafting while monitoring observes only executed actions with no visibility into the evolving simulation heuristics.

For example, an attacker selectively provides inputs that reinforce heuristics favoring data-transfer operations, causing an MCTS agent's rollout policy to gradually prefer exfiltration pathways over the course of hundreds of planning cycles, while monitoring dashboards show only per-step action logs with no rollout policy versioning or heuristic drift indicators.

Standard defenses such as behavioral baseline comparisons and action-rate anomaly detection are insufficient because rollout policy drift operates sub-symptomatically—executed actions remain within expected ranges while the underlying heuristic that generates them shifts incrementally toward attacker-controlled preferences.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In a single-agent deployment, policy drift affects one agent's future decisions; in multi-agent systems where agents share learned rollout policies or where one agent's outputs train another's heuristics, drift in one rollout policy propagates to the broader agent fleet, compounding across pipeline stages in ways that aggregate monitoring cannot attribute to the originating policy change.
