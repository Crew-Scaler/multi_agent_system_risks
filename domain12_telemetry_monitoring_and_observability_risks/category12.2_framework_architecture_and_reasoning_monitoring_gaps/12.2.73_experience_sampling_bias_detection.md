# 12.2.73 Experience Sampling Bias Detection [NoFW]

Deep reinforcement learning systems sample from experience buffers in patterns that reveal which experiences are prioritized, and monitoring these sampling patterns can in principle indicate which training focus areas are being manipulated; however, in multi-agent systems aggregated sampling across agents reveals aggregated training focus that obscures individual agent manipulation, and sophisticated adversaries exploit this by introducing sampling bias that averages out in aggregate telemetry.

For example, an attacker poisons the experience buffer of one agent in a ten-agent reinforcement learning fleet to over-sample failure states involving a specific tool, biasing that agent toward avoiding the tool; when monitoring aggregates experience sampling statistics across all ten agents, the biased agent's over-sampling averages with unaffected agents' distributions and falls within normal bounds, leaving the manipulation undetected.

Standard defenses such as replay buffer integrity checks and periodic experience distribution audits are insufficient because they examine aggregate distributions without decomposing sampling patterns to the individual agent level, allowing per-agent bias to remain invisible in fleet-level statistics.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Sampling bias in multi-agent reinforcement learning is structurally harder to detect than in single-agent systems because the fleet's aggregate sampling distribution is expected to be heterogeneous, providing natural cover for adversarially introduced per-agent biases that would be immediately apparent in a single-agent deployment where the full distribution belongs to one agent.
