# 12.2.67 Probability Distribution Shift Impact on Utility Decisions [NoFW]

Monitoring lacks visibility into how outcome probability distribution changes affect expected utility calculations; when probabilities shift due to market conditions, tool reliability degradation, or adversarial manipulation, monitoring continues tracking decision frequency without detecting that utility-calculation-fundamental changes have occurred, and in multi-agent systems probability distribution shifts can cause cascading recalculations across agent networks that monitoring does not correlate.

For example, an attacker subtly inflates the estimated success probability of a risky database write operation by poisoning the agent's historical outcome memory; the agent's utility calculation shifts toward approving riskier operations, but monitoring records only the final action decisions without capturing the probability estimates that drove them, making the manipulation invisible until failures accumulate.

Standard defenses such as anomaly detection on decision frequency distributions are insufficient because the probability shifts cause decisions to change in expected directions relative to the agent's (now corrupted) internal model, and action-level monitoring cannot distinguish between correctly responding to a genuine environmental shift and responding to adversarially induced probability distortion.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In multi-agent systems, adversarially shifted probability distributions propagate across agents that share outcome history or use upstream agents' outputs as probability inputs, creating correlated utility miscalculations that compound through the pipeline yet remain invisible at each individual agent's decision log.
