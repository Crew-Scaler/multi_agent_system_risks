# 12.2.76 Temporal Learning Dynamics Leaking Attack Signals [NoFW]

Learning curves (loss, reward progression) can leak attack signatures because poisoned training shows distinctive convergence patterns; monitoring learning curves can detect training-time attacks, but slow poisoning spread over many iterations hides attack signatures in noise, and in multi-agent systems aggregated learning dynamics across agents further hide individual attack signals by averaging poisoned convergence with unaffected agents' normal curves.

For example, an attacker introduces a backdoor into the training data of one agent in a twenty-agent fleet, producing a slightly atypical loss curve that deviates from normal convergence by less than one standard deviation per epoch; when monitoring aggregates loss curves across all agents to produce fleet-level convergence statistics, the poisoned agent's anomalous curve is averaged out by the nineteen unaffected agents, making the attack signature vanish in the aggregate.

Standard defenses such as per-agent learning curve monitoring with statistical anomaly detection are insufficient when poisoning is calibrated to produce gradual convergence shifts that remain within normal variance bounds for the entire training period, appearing as acceptable run-to-run variation rather than evidence of data corruption.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Temporal learning signal aggregation in multi-agent systems converts what would be an individually detectable anomalous convergence curve into a statistically normal aggregate, and the absence of per-agent learning dynamics visibility in fleet-level dashboards systematically eliminates the monitoring signal that would reveal the attack in a single-agent setting.
