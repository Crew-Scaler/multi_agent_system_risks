# 12.2.34 Quality Score Metric Blind Spots in Safety Monitoring [NoFW]

Custom quality metrics such as coherence and faithfulness scores assigned to reasoning paths are monitored as outcome metrics but not as targets of manipulation; inflated quality scores on compromised paths make those paths appear legitimate in safety monitoring while concealing injected content.

For example, an attacker embedding a reward-hacking prompt that causes the model's coherence scorer to assign maximum scores to malicious reasoning paths ensures those paths win the majority vote while the monitoring system observes high-quality outputs, raising no alerts despite the paths containing injected instructions.

Standard defenses such as threshold-based quality score monitoring and distribution anomaly detection are insufficient because they treat quality scores as ground truth rather than as adversarially manipulable proxies, and consistently inflated scores on compromised paths produce no distribution anomalies when the manipulation is systematic.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In cross-agent safety monitoring where quality metrics from heterogeneous agents are aggregated, an individual agent's score manipulation disappears into the combined distribution; single-agent quality metric monitoring can focus on one methodology and detect inflation patterns that multi-agent aggregation permanently conceals.
