# 12.2.52 Search Tree Expansion Rate Blinding [NoFW]

Monitoring systems alert on sudden increases in A* search tree expansion rates as a proxy for anomalous planning load; adversaries degrade heuristic quality incrementally to expand search trees gradually, staying within per-alert thresholds at individual agent level while aggregated multi-agent metrics obscure the compound degradation through averaging with unaffected agents.

For example, across a fleet of ten path-planning agents, an attacker degrades the admissibility of heuristics in three agents by 15% each cycle; the three affected agents expand trees 30% larger than baseline, but fleet-level averaging with the seven unaffected agents keeps the aggregate expansion metric within the configured alert band, hiding the compound degradation.

Standard defenses such as per-cycle node-expansion alerts and resource-usage thresholds are insufficient because they rely on detecting step-changes in expansion rates, which adversarial incremental heuristic degradation is specifically designed to avoid by staying beneath single-cycle detection thresholds.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In a single-agent deployment, expansion-rate monitoring operates on one agent's history, making per-agent baselines sensitive to incremental drift; in multi-agent fleet monitoring, individual agent degradation is obscured by aggregation with healthy agents, and attackers can calibrate per-agent degradation to remain undetectable even as compound computational cost and plan quality loss accumulate across the fleet.
