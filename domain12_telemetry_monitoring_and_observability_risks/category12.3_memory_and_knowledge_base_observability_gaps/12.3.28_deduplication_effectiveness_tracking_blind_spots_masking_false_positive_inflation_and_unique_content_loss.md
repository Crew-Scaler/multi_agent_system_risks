# 12.3.28 Deduplication Effectiveness Tracking Blind Spots Masking False Positive Inflation and Unique Content Loss [NoFW]

Deduplication monitoring tracks duplicate removal counts and aggregate rates without tracking false positive rates; when deduplication marks unique legitimate documents as duplicates and removes them, monitoring shows increased deduplication effectiveness (higher removal rate) while actual effect is content loss, and the absence of falsely-removed documents from the knowledge base goes undetected until users report missing information.

For example, when semantic deduplication with a 0.85 cosine-similarity threshold incorrectly marks two related but distinct product liability rulings as duplicates and removes one, monitoring shows "deduplication rate increased from 18% to 19%" as a positive signal while the removed ruling is permanently absent from legal agent retrieval until a lawyer reports a missing precedent.

Standard defenses such as deduplication rate monitoring and corpus size tracking are insufficient because they cannot distinguish beneficial duplicate removal from false positive content loss without per-pair similarity score audit trails showing which documents were treated as duplicates of which source.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In multi-agent systems where different agents specialize in distinct knowledge domains, false positive deduplication in one domain silently removes content relevant to that agent while agents in other domains remain unaffected; without per-agent deduplication audit logs, the shared knowledge store loses domain-specific content with no signal to the affected agent.
