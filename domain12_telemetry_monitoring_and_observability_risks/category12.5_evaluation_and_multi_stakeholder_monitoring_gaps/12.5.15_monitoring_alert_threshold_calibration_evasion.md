# 12.5.15 Monitoring Alert Threshold Calibration Evasion [NoFW]

Continuous evaluation monitoring uses static alert thresholds that adversaries can evade by causing gradual metric degradation that stays within threshold bounds; the cumulative effect of many small sub-threshold degradations across multiple metrics and agents exceeds the practical quality bounds without triggering any discrete alerts.

For example, an attacker who degrades each of ten quality metrics by 0.5% per week across ten agents produces a compound system-level degradation of 5% per metric after ten weeks—well beyond acceptable bounds—while no individual agent ever triggers an alert because each agent's individual weekly change of 0.5% remains below its 1% alerting threshold.

Standard defenses such as per-metric alert thresholds and weekly trend reports are insufficient because they evaluate each metric and each agent independently, and gradual degradation that stays below any individual threshold produces no alert even as the cumulative compound effect renders system-level quality unacceptable.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In single-agent systems compound degradation across all metrics eventually triggers the agent's own alerts; in multi-agent systems with independent per-agent thresholds, coordinated sub-threshold degradation distributed across agents produces no alert in any individual agent's monitoring while accumulating into a system-level quality failure that no single stakeholder's dashboard reveals.
