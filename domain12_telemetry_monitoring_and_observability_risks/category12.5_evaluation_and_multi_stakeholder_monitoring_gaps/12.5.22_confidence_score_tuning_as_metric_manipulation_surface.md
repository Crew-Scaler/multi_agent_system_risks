# 12.5.22 Confidence Score Tuning as Metric Manipulation Surface [NoFW]

Agents can be tuned with sampling parameters (temperature, top-p) specifically to appear well-calibrated in monitoring dashboards while producing systematically wrong high-confidence outputs; monitoring that compares confidence to observed accuracy is deceived by parameter choices that produce high-confidence incorrect answers.

For example, an agent configured with a low temperature and constrained sampling vocabulary produces outputs that appear maximally confident in monitoring dashboards because the narrow sampling distribution generates high probability scores, yet the same configuration causes the agent to confidently assert incorrect answers drawn from a biased subset of its vocabulary that the temperature reduction made dominant.

Standard defenses such as confidence calibration evaluation and confidence-accuracy correlation dashboards are insufficient because they measure whether confidence correlates with accuracy on observed outputs, but an attacker who tunes parameters to produce high-confidence incorrect answers in specific underrepresented categories can maintain overall correlation metrics while gaming accuracy in the targeted category.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Single-agent confidence monitoring tracks one agent's calibration surface; multi-agent systems aggregate confidence across agents, and a subset of agents tuned to inflate confidence in specific domains shifts aggregate confidence metrics in ways that no per-agent monitor detects, because each individual agent's confidence distribution passes local calibration checks while collectively distorting the system-level confidence signal.
