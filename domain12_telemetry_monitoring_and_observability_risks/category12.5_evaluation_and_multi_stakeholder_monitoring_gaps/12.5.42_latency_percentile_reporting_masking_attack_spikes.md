# 12.5.42 Latency Percentile Reporting Masking Attack Spikes [NoFW]

Efficiency monitoring reports latency at p50, p95, and p99 percentiles; attack-induced latency spikes affecting only 0.5% of operations fall below the p99 reporting threshold and remain invisible in standard percentile dashboards, requiring p99.5 or maximum latency monitoring that is typically not configured.

For example, an adversary triggers excessive computation in a document processing agent for queries containing rare Unicode sequences, causing 0.3% of requests to experience 10-second latency spikes; standard monitoring reporting p99 latency at 800ms shows no anomaly because only p99.7 and above are affected, allowing the adversary to intermittently exhaust processing capacity for affected requests while latency dashboards continue to show healthy percentile values.

Standard defenses such as p50/p95/p99 latency dashboards and standard SLA thresholds are insufficient because percentile reporting is a smoothing operation that hides extreme tail events by design, and monitoring configured around common SLA thresholds has no visibility into sub-percentile attack effects.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In multi-agent systems, tail latency attacks can target a single agent's latency distribution while aggregate fleet percentile reporting further dilutes the signal; an attack causing 0.5% of one agent's operations to spike may represent only 0.01% of fleet-wide operations when averaged across a 50-agent fleet, making the attack invisible in aggregate percentile dashboards even if p99.5 were monitored at the fleet level.
