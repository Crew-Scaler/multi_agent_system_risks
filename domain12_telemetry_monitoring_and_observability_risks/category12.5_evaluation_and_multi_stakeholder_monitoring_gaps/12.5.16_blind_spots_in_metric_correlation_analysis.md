# 12.5.16 Blind Spots in Metric Correlation Analysis [NoFW]

Evaluation monitoring analyzes each metric independently without computing cross-metric correlations; coordinated attacks that simultaneously degrade multiple metrics in patterns that appear statistically uncorrelated when analyzed individually reveal their adversarial coordination only through multivariate correlation analysis that current monitoring does not perform.

For example, an attacker who simultaneously reduces accuracy by 2%, safety compliance by 2%, and response consistency by 2% across three separate evaluation agents produces three independent metric movements that each look like normal statistical variation when analyzed individually, while multivariate correlation analysis would immediately identify the synchronized cross-metric decline as anomalous.

Standard defenses such as per-metric threshold alerts and individual metric trend monitoring are insufficient because they treat metrics as independent variables and cannot detect the coordinated nature of simultaneous multi-metric degradation that characterizes attacks exploiting the independence assumption.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Single-agent monitoring operates over one metric stream where correlation analysis is straightforward; multi-agent monitoring aggregates metrics across agents with independent reporting schedules and different operational baselines, making cross-agent, cross-metric correlation computationally expensive and organizationally complex in ways that current monitoring does not address.
