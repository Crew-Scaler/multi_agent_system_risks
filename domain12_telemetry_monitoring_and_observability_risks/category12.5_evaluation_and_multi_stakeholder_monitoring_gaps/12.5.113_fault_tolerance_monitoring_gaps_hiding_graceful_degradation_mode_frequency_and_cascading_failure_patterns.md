# 12.5.113 Fault Tolerance Monitoring Gaps Hiding Graceful Degradation Mode Frequency and Cascading Failure Patterns [NoFW]

Circuit breaker monitoring reports current state (open or closed) without tracking how frequently circuits open, how long degraded modes persist, or whether one circuit opening causally triggers others; operators see a closed circuit and assume healthy operation while missing that the circuit oscillates open/closed dozens of times daily or that cascading circuit failures occur across dependent services.

For example, a circuit breaker that opens and closes repeatedly in a 10-minute window due to deliberate load injection appears healthy in a monitoring dashboard that polls circuit state every 5 minutes; each poll observes the closed state during a recovery interval, completely missing the oscillation pattern and the downstream service degradations each opening triggered.

Standard defenses such as circuit breaker state dashboards and service health checks are insufficient because they capture instantaneous state rather than state transition history and frequency, missing attack patterns that produce rapid oscillation or cascading failures that resolve faster than monitoring polling intervals.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In multi-agent systems, a circuit breaker opening in one agent's dependency can force workload redistribution to other agents whose circuits subsequently open under elevated load; this cascading pattern is invisible in per-agent circuit-state monitoring that shows each agent's current state without capturing the causal propagation chain that connects circuit failures across agent boundaries.
