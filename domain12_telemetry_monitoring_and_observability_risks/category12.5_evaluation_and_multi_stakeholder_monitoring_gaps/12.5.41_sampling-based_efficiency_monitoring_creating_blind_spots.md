# 12.5.41 Sampling-Based Efficiency Monitoring Creating Blind Spots [NoFW]

High-volume systems monitor efficiency metrics through sampling (1% of operations), allowing adversaries to craft attacks that trigger only under conditions not covered by the sampling strategy; malicious efficiency behaviors present in the unsampled 99% of operations remain undetected indefinitely.

For example, an adversary who learns that efficiency monitoring samples every 100th operation crafts a malicious prompt pattern that executes only on operations numbered 51 through 99 between sampled checkpoints, guaranteeing that the attack-triggered resource consumption is never captured by the sampling-based monitor while operating on 49% of all traffic.

Standard defenses such as sampled distributed tracing and percentage-based monitoring are insufficient because sampling strategies are typically documented in configuration files that determined adversaries can discover, and once sampling boundaries are known, adversaries can design attack timing to avoid monitored operations systematically.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In multi-agent systems, sampling blind spots compound across independent agents: if each agent independently samples 1% of operations, the probability that a coordinated cross-agent attack sequence is captured by all agents simultaneously drops to near zero, enabling coordinated attacks to execute in unsampled windows across agent boundaries while each agent's monitoring appears healthy within its own sampled operations.
