# 12.5.39 Attribution and Traceability Gaps in Reasoning Chains [NoFW]

Multi-agent systems lack mechanisms to trace reasoning contributions back to specific agents; when emergent reasoning failures occur at agent handoffs, the absence of per-agent reasoning attribution prevents identification of which agent introduced the reasoning error, enabling adversaries who inject corrupted reasoning into intermediate agents to hide in attribution ambiguity.

For example, in a legal document analysis MAS where a clause extraction agent passes summaries to an interpretation agent and then to a compliance assessment agent, a flawed legal interpretation surfaces in the final compliance report; because reasoning logs show only inputs and outputs at each stage without provenance, operators cannot determine whether the error originated in clause extraction, in the interpretation step, or in compliance assessment, allowing a targeted injection into the interpretation agent to permanently escape attribution.

Standard defenses such as chain-of-thought logging and step-level audit trails are insufficient because they record what reasoning occurred at each agent without linking each reasoning fragment to its upstream source, so when reasoning errors emerge after multiple handoffs, attribution requires manual correlation of logs across agents with no shared reasoning provenance framework.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Attribution ambiguity is structurally absent in single-agent systems, where the complete reasoning chain is produced by one model and every inference step is internally attributable; in multi-agent systems, reasoning contributions are distributed across independent agents without a shared provenance model, creating an attribution gap that grows with pipeline depth and enables adversaries to target intermediate agents with high confidence of evading attribution.
