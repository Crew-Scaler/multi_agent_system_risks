# 12.5.77 Profiling Data Retention and Forensic Gaps [NoFW]

GPU and CPU profiling data is retained briefly due to storage constraints, creating windows where attacks occurring between profiling periods leave no forensic evidence; in multi-agent systems where profiling decisions are made at fleet level, adversaries who understand the profiling schedule can time attacks to occur between profiling windows across all agents simultaneously.

For example, an attacker who has identified that a production multi-agent system retains GPU profiling data for only 24 hours executes a covert data exfiltration operation over a weekend period, ensuring the attack activity falls outside the retention window before the Monday morning security review; by the time investigators attempt forensic reconstruction, all profiling evidence has been overwritten.

Standard defenses such as extended retention policies and cold storage archival are insufficient because profiling data volumes from multi-agent GPU fleets accumulate at rates that make retention beyond 24–48 hours economically infeasible without dedicated archival infrastructure, and attackers who monitor retention-related operational procedures can reliably identify the coverage window.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In a single-agent system, the profiling data volume is bounded by one process's GPU activity; in a multi-agent fleet, the combined profiling volume across all agents makes extended retention prohibitively expensive, creating a retention coverage gap that attackers who understand fleet-level profiling economics can reliably exploit by timing attacks to the post-retention window.
