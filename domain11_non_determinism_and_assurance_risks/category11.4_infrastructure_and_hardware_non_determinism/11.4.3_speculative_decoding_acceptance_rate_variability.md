# 11.4.3 Speculative Decoding Acceptance Rate Variability [NoFW]

Variable acceptance rates in speculative decoding produce non-deterministic token output distributions that are difficult to characterize for safety assurance.

Speculative decoding pairs a small draft model with a larger verifier model: the draft generates candidate tokens that the verifier accepts or rejects probabilistically based on the ratio of their respective probability estimates; the acceptance rate varies with input vocabulary distribution and sequence entropy, causing the effective generation path to switch non-deterministically between draft-extended continuations and verifier-generated ones. An attacker calibrates a prompt to the high-acceptance-rate regime, causing the model to follow the draft model's less-scrutinized generation path for an extended token sequence; under this regime, safety guardrails that rely on the verifier's token-by-token evaluation are bypassed because the verifier passively accepts rather than regenerating. For example, an injection crafted against a deployment where acceptance rates average 0.85 achieves harmful completions in 40% of trials, but the same injection against a deployment with acceptance rates of 0.55—where the verifier frequently rejects and regenerates—succeeds in fewer than 2% of trials, making safety testing on one deployment's configuration non-transferable to another.

Standard defenses such as output filtering and safety fine-tuning are insufficient because the token sequence structure produced under high-acceptance-rate conditions differs from sequences the verifier generates autonomously, and filters calibrated primarily on verifier-generated outputs may not cover the vocabulary and phrasing patterns the draft model favors at high acceptance rates.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In MAS, agents routed through different inference replicas with different speculative decoding configurations will receive structurally divergent completions from the same prompt; when one agent's completion serves as the input context for a downstream agent, acceptance-rate-induced token divergence propagates through the pipeline, producing final outputs that are sensitive to the acceptance-rate regime of every upstream agent—a source of non-determinism that single-agent deployments using a single inference backend never encounter.
