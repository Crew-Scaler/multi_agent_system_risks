# 11.6.12 Experience Replay Temporal Non-Determinism [NoFW]

Deep RL systems sample experiences from replay buffers with temporal gaps, causing the same state to produce different policy updates depending on which historical experiences are sampled. Shared replay buffers create correlated temporal non-determinism across all agents drawing from the same experience pool.

For example, two agents sharing a replay buffer that includes both pre-incident and post-incident experiences may on the same training step sample different temporal slices: one agent draws primarily recent post-incident data and updates toward cautious behavior, while the other draws older pre-incident data and reinforces aggressive behavior—producing two agents with divergent policies that were ostensibly trained on the same shared dataset.

Standard defenses such as prioritized experience replay and temporal sampling strategies are insufficient because the non-determinism arises from stochastic sampling itself; even deterministic priority functions cannot eliminate variance in which experiences are co-sampled across a mini-batch, and shared buffers introduce cross-agent correlations that amplify rather than reduce this variance.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In a MAS using shared replay buffers, one agent's sampling non-determinism creates correlated policy divergence across all agents; non-determinism in temporal experience selection propagates through coordination interfaces to destabilize the behavior of agents that depend on consistent policy assumptions, creating synchronized divergence unavailable in single-agent replay systems.
