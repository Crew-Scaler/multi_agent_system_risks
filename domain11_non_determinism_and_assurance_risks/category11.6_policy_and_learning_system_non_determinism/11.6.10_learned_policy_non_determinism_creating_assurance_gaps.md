# 11.6.10 Learned Policy Non-Determinism Creating Assurance Gaps [NoFW]

Learning-based policies contain stochastic components (softmax action selection, dropout, temperature sampling). Identical states produce different actions, and multi-agent policy non-determinism compounds creating exponential behavior variance that makes comprehensive pre-deployment assurance infeasible.

For example, a fleet of reinforcement-learning agents each using softmax action selection at temperature T=0.8 will, when presented with the same ambiguous state, sample different actions across the fleet; a downstream orchestrator that aggregates their recommendations cannot derive a stable consensus, and the resulting collective decision is sensitive to the precise mix of random draws across agents at that moment rather than to the merits of the options.

Standard defenses such as deterministic evaluation modes (temperature=0 at inference) and ensemble averaging are insufficient because deterministic modes are often disabled in production to maintain exploration or calibration benefits, and ensemble averaging masks individual-agent variance rather than eliminating it, allowing correlated tail behavior to still produce dangerous collective decisions.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In a MAS, independently stochastic policies compound across agents; non-determinism in one agent's action sampling propagates through coordination interfaces to destabilize the behavior of agents that depend on stable policy outputs, creating exponential joint-behavior variance unavailable in single-agent stochastic policy systems.
