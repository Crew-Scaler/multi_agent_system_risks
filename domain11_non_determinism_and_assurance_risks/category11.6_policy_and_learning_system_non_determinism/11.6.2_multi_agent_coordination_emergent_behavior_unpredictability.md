# 11.6.2 Multi-Agent Coordination Emergent Behavior Unpredictability [NoFW]

Independently learned policies in coordinating agents produce emergent collective behaviors that are not predictable from any individual agent's policy.

Multi-agent reinforcement learning systems develop individual policies through training, but when those policies interact at runtime they produce collective behaviors—turn-taking patterns, resource-allocation norms, implicit signaling conventions—that were never exhibited during single-agent evaluation and cannot be inferred from any one agent's policy in isolation. For example, two trading agents trained separately to maximize portfolio returns may converge in deployment on a correlated strategy that amplifies market volatility, a behavior observable only when both agents are co-executing against the same live order book. An attacker who studies the individual policies in isolation cannot predict these emergent joint strategies, but can craft environmental inputs that predictably trigger destabilizing joint responses once the agents' learned coordination patterns are reverse-engineered.

Standard defenses such as unit-testing individual agent policies or certifying agents in isolation are insufficient because emergent behaviors arise precisely from agent interactions and are absent in any single-agent test environment; the combinatorial space of joint executions makes exhaustive assurance computationally infeasible.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In a MAS, independently trained agents may converge on mutually reinforcing policies that produce joint behaviors neither agent was trained to exhibit; non-determinism in one agent's policy update propagates through coordination interfaces to destabilize the behavior of agents that depend on stable policy contracts, creating emergent instability unavailable in single-agent policy systems.
