# 11.6.6 Non-Deterministic Weight Adjustment Mechanisms Creating Unsafe Adaptation [NoFW]

Systems dynamically adjusting utility weights based on outcomes face non-deterministic convergence. Multi-agent systems enable divergent convergence where agents develop incompatible utility functions, creating collective decision-making behavior that no single agent's evaluation could predict.

For example, two safety-critical agents that start with identical multi-objective utility functions—balancing throughput, latency, and safety margin—may, after processing different operational streams, converge on weight configurations that prioritize throughput differently; when they coordinate on a joint decision, each agent's evaluation of the same option differs, leading to inconsistent approval signals that an orchestrator breaks by defaulting to the least conservative choice.

Standard defenses such as bounded weight-update rates or periodic re-synchronization are insufficient because weight adjustment occurs continuously between synchronization points, and an adversary can submit a targeted sequence of outcomes that steers one agent's weights toward an unsafe configuration before the next synchronization event.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In a MAS, independently adjusting agents may converge on conflicting utility functions; non-determinism in one agent's weight adjustment propagates through coordination interfaces to destabilize the behavior of agents that depend on stable utility contracts, creating divergent collective evaluation unavailable in single-agent weight adjustment systems.
