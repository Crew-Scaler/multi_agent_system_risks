# 11.6.7 Evaluation Non-Reproducibility for Utility-Based Decisions [NoFW]

Agents making decisions through expected utility optimization face non-reproducible testing. Testing reveals safe decisions under one sampled distribution, but identical future inputs trigger unsafe decisions when the utility-sampling distribution shifts, making pre-deployment safety evaluation unreliable for utility-based agents.

For example, a medical-triage agent validated on a historical patient distribution correctly refuses to escalate low-acuity cases; after deployment, a subtle demographic shift in incoming patients changes the underlying outcome distribution, and the same symptom profile that consistently triggered the safe "monitor" action during testing now non-deterministically triggers "escalate" or "discharge" depending on which sampled rollout the agent happens to draw.

Standard defenses such as held-out test sets and shadow-mode evaluation are insufficient because they evaluate the agent against a fixed historical distribution; any distribution shift post-deployment invalidates the test findings, and the shift itself may be imperceptible until the unsafe behavior is already manifesting in production.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In a MAS, each agent's utility-sampling distribution can shift independently; non-determinism in one agent's evaluation propagates through coordination interfaces to destabilize the behavior of agents that depend on consistent utility signals, creating cross-agent evaluation divergence unavailable in single-agent utility systems.
