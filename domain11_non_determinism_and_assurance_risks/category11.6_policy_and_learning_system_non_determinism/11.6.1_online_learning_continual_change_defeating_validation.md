# 11.6.1 Online Learning Continual Change Defeating Validation [NoFW]

Agents learning from operational experience continuously update their policies, invalidating any prior safety validation and creating a moving target for security assurance.

Online learning agents update their policies continuously from new operational data, so a policy validated at deployment may behave fundamentally differently within hours as gradient updates accumulate. For example, a content-moderation agent that passes all pre-release safety tests begins reclassifying borderline inputs differently after processing live traffic; the safety certificate signed at deployment time no longer describes the agent's behavior a day later. The attack surface this creates is not theoretical: an adversary can submit a carefully sequenced stream of interaction data that gradually steers the policy toward a target behavior while each incremental update remains too small to trigger monitoring alerts.

Standard defenses such as periodic re-validation and safety checkpoints are insufficient because re-validation only covers the policy at the moment of the checkpoint, and a fast-updating agent may drift significantly between checks before the next scheduled review.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In a multi-agent system, independently online-learning agents can reinforce each other's drift—one agent's updated outputs become training signals for a downstream agent, meaning non-determinism in one agent's policy update propagates through coordination interfaces to destabilize the behavior of agents that depend on stable policy contracts, creating emergent instability unavailable in single-agent policy systems.
