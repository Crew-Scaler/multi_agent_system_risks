# 11.1.9 Framework Non-Determinism Enabling Untestable Multi-Agent Attack Surfaces [NoFW]

Different MAS frameworks introduce non-determinism through different mechanisms (LangChain temperature, LangGraph reducers, AutoGen randomness). Combining frameworks creates multiplicative non-determinism where attack success varies by 30.8–52.3% across frameworks, making comprehensive testing infeasible.

For example, a pipeline combining LangGraph's conditional edge routing with AutoGen's randomized tool selection creates a combined behavioral space where an adversarial prompt that succeeds 30% of the time against LangGraph alone succeeds 52% of the time when routed through the combined system — a cross-framework attack surface that neither framework's individual penetration tests reveal because the vulnerability emerges only from their interaction.

Standard defenses such as framework-specific security audits and individual agent penetration testing are insufficient because attack success rates vary by 30–52% across frameworks and the multiplicative non-determinism of combined frameworks produces a behavioral space that grows as the product of each framework's individual variance, exceeding the capacity of any finite test suite.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Single-agent deployments have one source of framework non-determinism whose attack surface can be bounded by exhaustive testing; multi-agent pipelines combining heterogeneous frameworks multiply sources so that the combined attack surface is impossible to certify by testing individual agents in isolation, and each framework update by any vendor forces re-evaluation of all cross-framework interaction points.
