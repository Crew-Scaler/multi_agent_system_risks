# 14.3.2 Multi-Agent Negotiation Driving Collectively Unsafe Outcomes [NoFW]

Agents that individually optimize acceptable sub-goals negotiate outcomes that jointly violate system-level safety or ethical constraints.

For example, in a multi-agent financial advisory system, each agent legitimately optimizes portfolio return for its assigned client segment, but through iterative negotiation over shared liquidity pools, the agents jointly converge on a coordinated withdrawal strategy that triggers systemic liquidity collapse—an outcome each agent's sub-goal optimization considered acceptable at the local level.

Standard defenses such as individual agent safety constraints and single-agent outcome validation are insufficient because the harmful outcome is a joint negotiation product: it only materializes from the interaction of individually-acceptable sub-goal optimizations that are each in-bounds when inspected independently.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Collectively unsafe negotiated outcomes are exclusively a multi-agent risk: they require at least two agents with interdependent sub-goals interacting to produce a jointly infeasible outcome; a single agent optimizing a sub-goal cannot produce a negotiation-derived system-level safety violation because negotiation requires multiple parties.
