# 10.6.27 RBAC Privilege Escalation Within Agent Service Accounts [NoFW]

If service account RBAC is misconfigured granting overly broad permissions, attackers compromising one agent service account can leverage it to manipulate other agents' configurations. This converts a single compromised agent's access into control over multiple agents through RBAC privilege chains.

For example, an attacker who compromises a data-ingestion agent whose service account holds a permissive `pods/exec` ClusterRole binding uses that access to open an exec session into the orchestrator agent's pod, reads its environment variables containing API keys for downstream tool services, and uses those credentials to issue tool calls on behalf of the orchestrator — escalating from data-ingestion scope to orchestrator-level authority through a single RBAC misassignment.

Standard defenses such as RBAC policy audits and OPA Gatekeeper admission policies are insufficient because permissive RBAC bindings frequently accumulate during development as engineers broaden permissions to debug issues and then forget to tighten them; furthermore, lateral movement through `pods/exec` or `secrets/get` does not itself trigger the admission webhooks that guard resource creation, making the escalation path invisible to creation-focused policy controls.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In single-agent deployments, RBAC misconfiguration exposes one workload's scope and remediation requires correcting one service account binding; in multi-agent systems where service accounts are often shared across agent types for operational simplicity, one overly permissive binding simultaneously grants the attacker traversal authority over every agent type sharing that account, enabling lateral movement to reconfigure, read secrets from, or inject instructions into the entire governed agent population through a single exploited binding.
