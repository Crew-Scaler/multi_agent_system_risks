# 10.6.1 Kubernetes Service Account Identity Spoofing Enabling Lateral Movement [NoFW]

Attackers compromise one agent's Kubernetes service account token and use it to impersonate that agent's identity across the cluster.

Kubernetes service accounts authenticate pod identity through tokens bound to service account credentials, but shared service accounts across multi-agent deployments create identity pooling where a single compromised token grants access to the identity of every agent using that account. When a production deployment assigns all inference agents to a single `agent-worker` service account for operational simplicity, an attacker extracting that token from one compromised pod can immediately impersonate every other agent, issuing API calls to cluster resources, secrets stores, and inter-agent communication channels without requiring per-agent credentials. The extracted token replays successfully from external sources, enabling persistent impersonation even after the originally compromised pod is terminated.

Standard defenses such as role-based access control and network policies are insufficient because they grant permissions to the service account identity rather than the individual pod, and token extraction bypasses these controls entirely once credentials are obtained from any one agent.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In single-agent deployments, a compromised service account token affects only that one agent's operations; in multi-agent fleets where dozens or hundreds of agents share a common service account, one token extraction enables attackers to impersonate the entire fleet simultaneously, with all downstream services and agents accepting those requests as legitimate.
