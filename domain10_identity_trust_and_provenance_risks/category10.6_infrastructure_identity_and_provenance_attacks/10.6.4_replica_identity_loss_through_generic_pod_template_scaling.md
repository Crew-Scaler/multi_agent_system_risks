# 10.6.4 Replica Identity Loss Through Generic Pod Template Scaling [NoFW]

Generic Kubernetes pod templates used for agent scaling do not bind individual replicas to verifiable identities, making it impossible to distinguish legitimate from attacker-injected replicas.

Kubernetes Deployment and ReplicaSet controllers generate pod replicas from a single pod template, assigning only a suffix-randomized name (e.g., `agent-worker-7d9f4b`) that carries no verifiable binding to hardware, cryptographic identity, or provenance. All replicas expose the same labels, annotations, and service account, making them functionally anonymous members of a set. An attacker who compromises one replica and modifies its inference weights or tool-call behavior produces a pod that is operationally indistinguishable from its legitimate siblings—same name pattern, same service account token class, same labels—and survives rolling restarts that cycle out legitimate pods while the compromised replica is rescheduled from the same poisoned image.

Standard defenses such as Kubernetes liveness probes and readiness checks are insufficient because they verify that a replica responds to health-check requests, not that it executes the intended model weights or tool logic, leaving behavioral compromise undetected.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Named, stateful single-agent deployments have individually auditable identities tied to configuration records; anonymous replica pools used in multi-agent systems eliminate per-instance accountability, meaning a compromised replica serves legitimate workloads alongside uncompromised peers indefinitely without identity-based detection.
