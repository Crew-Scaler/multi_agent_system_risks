# 10.6.25 Certificate Rotation Desynchronization as Trust Chain Attack [NoFW]

Fleet Command uses automatically rotating X.509 certificates to authenticate edge agents. An attacker partially compromising the certificate authority can desynchronize certificate rotation across agents, creating windows where some agents accept certificates from malicious sources while rotation timing mismatches prevent synchronized revocation across the fleet.

For example, an attacker who partially compromises Fleet Command's certificate authority delays rotation notifications to a subset of agents while issuing a fraudulent certificate timed to expire after the legitimate agents' rotation window; the out-of-sync agents, still trusting the old CA certificate, accept the fraudulent credential during the rotation gap, while the synchronized agents have already revoked it — creating a split-brain trust state where some agents communicate securely and others accept attacker-controlled connections.

Standard defenses such as automated certificate rotation schedules and OCSP stapling are insufficient because rotation schedules assume reliable delivery of revocation and issuance notifications to all agents simultaneously; network partitions, edge connectivity gaps, and processing delays in large fleets routinely create desynchronization windows, and an attacker who can induce even modest timing skew across the fleet can exploit these windows without needing persistent CA access.

**Multi-agent distinction:** In single-agent deployments, certificate rotation affects one endpoint and desynchronization is a transient bilateral issue; in multi-agent fleets with thousands of independently rotating agents, partial CA compromise creates a heterogeneous trust state where different agents trust different certificate generations simultaneously — enabling attackers to exploit the rotation gap to present certificates that pass validation for the lagging agents while remaining invisible to already-rotated agents, attacking precisely the devices that cannot yet act on the revocation.
