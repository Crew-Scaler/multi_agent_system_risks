# 10.6.3 Auto-Scaling Triggered Identity Discontinuity [NoFW]

Forcing auto-scaling launches new replicas with fresh identities, creating apparent service continuity breaks that attackers use to insert malicious replicas during the scaling event.

Auto-scaling systems launch new agent replicas dynamically in response to load triggers, but newly provisioned replicas carry fresh identities with no established behavioral history, creating windows during which legitimate and attacker-injected replicas are indistinguishable. An attacker who can artificially inflate load metrics—by flooding request queues or manipulating monitoring data—can trigger scale-out events and simultaneously register attacker-controlled pods with valid-looking names during the registration window when the cluster orchestrator accepts new replica announcements. The resulting fleet contains a mix of legitimate and malicious replicas, all appearing identical to the load balancer and to orchestrating agents.

Standard defenses such as pod admission controllers and image signature verification are insufficient because they validate the image origin but cannot prevent an attacker who has already compromised the image registry or the admission webhook itself from injecting malicious replicas during the scaling event.

**Multi-agent distinction:** A static single-agent deployment has a stable, auditable identity over time; multi-agent auto-scaling fleets are in constant identity flux as replicas appear and disappear, creating recurring insertion opportunities where each scaling event is a potential identity injection point affecting all agents that route work to the newly scaled pool.
