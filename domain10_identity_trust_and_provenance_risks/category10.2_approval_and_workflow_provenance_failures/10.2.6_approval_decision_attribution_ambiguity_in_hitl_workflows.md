# 10.2.6 Approval Decision Attribution Ambiguity in HITL Workflows [NoFW]

Human-in-the-loop approval workflows create ambiguous attribution when multiple actors contribute to final decisions. Multi-agent contexts amplify this because approval chains span multiple agents with different policies—attribution fragments across agent boundaries. Mitigation requires explicit responsibility assignment, modification logging, batch approval granularity tracking, and complete decision chain reconstruction.

For example, in a financial transaction approval workflow where Agent A performs risk analysis, Agent B flags compliance issues, and a human approver clicks approval based on the aggregated recommendation, a subsequent audit cannot establish whether the approval was based on Agent A's manipulated risk score, Agent B's independently formed compliance judgment, or the human's personal assessment—leaving accountability for a fraudulent approval unresolvable across all three contributing parties.

Standard defenses such as digital signatures on human approval actions are insufficient because they authenticate that a human approved at a given time but not which agent analyses the human relied upon, leaving the causal chain from agent output to human decision unattested and post-incident attribution impossible.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Single-agent HITL workflows have a binary attribution model—human or agent; multi-agent HITL workflows fragment attribution across multiple contributing agents and a human approver, creating accountability gaps at every handoff point that compound with the number of agents in the chain and make post-incident responsibility assignment structurally ambiguous.
