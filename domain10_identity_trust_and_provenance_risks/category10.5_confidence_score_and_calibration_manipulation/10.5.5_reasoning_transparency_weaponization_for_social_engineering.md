# 10.5.5 Reasoning Transparency Weaponization for Social Engineering [NoFW]

Agents with high reasoning transparency create stronger social engineering vectors when reasoning is publicly visible to users who trust explicit logic. A medical agent showing transparent reasoning creates an appearance of scientific rigor even when that reasoning contains unsupported claims. In healthcare systems with multiple diagnostic agents, user trust in one agent's transparent reasoning can override legitimate safety mechanisms of other agents.

For example, a diagnostic agent whose chain-of-thought cites specific laboratory reference ranges and clinical guidelines—even when those citations are subtly manipulated—projects authority that causes a clinician to override a safety flag raised by a less-transparent downstream risk-scoring agent.

Standard defenses such as peer review by a second agent are insufficient because the transparent reasoning of the first agent primes reviewers to assume correctness; the cognitive anchoring effect of explicit logic makes reviewers less likely to challenge conclusions that appear well-supported rather than evaluating them independently.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Confidence scores propagate as inter-agent trust signals; a single agent's high-transparency reasoning not only influences human reviewers directly but is adopted as context by downstream agents in the pipeline, causing its unverified premises to compound through automated reasoning chains that treat the transparent logic as a trusted prior rather than a claim requiring independent validation.
