# 10.5.7 Benchmark Attribution Spoofing Creating False Reputation Provenance [NoFW]

Benchmark results are attributed to specific agents/versions, but attackers can spoof attribution causing malicious agent improvements to be credited to honest agents. Multi-agent systems with transitive reputation relationships enable false attribution creating cascading trust exploitation where spoofed benchmark provenance translates into unearned deployment trust.

For example, an attacker publishes benchmark results under the identifier of a well-regarded honest agent, causing downstream agent-selection logic to deploy the malicious variant whenever the system seeks the top-performing agent; the malicious agent then inherits the honest agent's routing priority and trust level throughout the pipeline.

Standard defenses such as version-pinned deployment registries are insufficient because the spoofing occurs at the benchmark attribution layer before deployment records are created; once a false performance record is accepted, downstream selection logic treats the fraudulent credentials as legitimate provenance.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Confidence scores propagate as inter-agent trust signals; spoofed benchmark attribution inflates the apparent credibility of a malicious agent, causing its outputs to be weighted more heavily across the entire pipeline and its inflated confidence scores to bypass human review thresholds that honest-but-lower-ranked agents would have triggered, compounding the reputational fraud through every downstream trust relationship.
