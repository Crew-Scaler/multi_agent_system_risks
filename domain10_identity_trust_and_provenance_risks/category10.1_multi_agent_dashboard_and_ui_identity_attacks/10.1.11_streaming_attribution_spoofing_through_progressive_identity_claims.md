# 10.1.11 Streaming Attribution Spoofing Through Progressive Identity Claims [NoFW]

Streaming responses enable identity spoofing where early tokens claim identity ("I am the Security Agent") while later tokens execute malicious operations before verification can occur. Multi-agent dashboards displaying streaming with identity attribution are vulnerable because early tokens establish trust before the complete stream enables identity verification.

Standard defenses such as message authentication and session tokens are insufficient because they authenticate the originating agent at the transport layer but cannot detect that early streaming tokens claim a false identity within the content stream itself—an assertion that resolves only after the user has already acted on the initial attribution and the attacker has established the trust needed to execute malicious operations in later tokens.

**Multi-agent distinction:** Single-agent streaming presents output under a stable, known identity; multi-agent streaming dashboards attribute streamed output to different agents dynamically, making early identity claims cognitively salient and actionable before the complete stream reveals potential contradictions—a spoofing surface that does not exist when only one agent produces content and identity is never in question.
